This is a business case/game designed to simulate the negotiations that take place between buyer and seller 
when purchasing a business. It is intended for students in a University business tax class, both undergradute
and graduate.

Participants can assume the role of either buyer or seller, and are playing "against" the computer. 
However, this is not a typical negotiation game. Rather, the parties use the art of collaborative negotiation by turning 
your opponent into a partner. (For more information on this, see Negotiation Strategies taught by Dr. Daylian Cain, Yale School of Management.)

The simulation takes the participant through three rounds of negotiations.
The parties have agreed to a taxable asset purchase as a starting point. Both parties then try to improve on the 
outcome ny negotiating changes to the deal. The parties negotiate by changing the purchase price, taking into
account buyer and seller may have different tax attributes, and the parties must find a price 
that works for both of them. 

Once the parties have completed the negotiation, they enter into a final bonus round where they explain the tax 
consequnces of the negotiated.
